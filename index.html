Privacy Policy

LetsCodeJS by SIXARS Studios  |  Effective October 22, 2026

LetsCodeJS is a mobile code editor for building and previewing projects on your phone in React, React Native, Vue, Angular or vanilla JavaScript. This policy explains what information the app handles, where it goes, and the choices you have. We wrote it to be read, so it avoids jargon where it can.

The short version

Who we are

LetsCodeJS is developed by Onaji Stephen Onche, trading as SIXARS Studios (“we”, “us”). If you have questions about this policy, see Contact.

What stays on your device

The following is created and stored locally on your phone. It is not uploaded to us.

  • Projects and code: project names, files, file contents, installed package lists, and which project you last had open.
  • Assets you add: images, audio, video or other files you choose with the photo or file picker. A copy is saved inside the app’s storage.
  • Preferences: your chosen theme.
  • Purchase status: if you buy the one-time lifetime unlock, the app keeps a record of that purchase (product ID and transaction ID) in your device’s secure storage so it can unlock features without asking you again.
  • Expo access token (optional): if you choose to sign in to Expo, the token is saved in your device’s secure storage (Android Keystore or iOS Keychain).

Because we hold none of this, we cannot recover it if you uninstall the app or lose your phone. Export your projects if you want a backup.

Third-party services

LetsCodeJS relies on outside services for ads, payments and package downloads. Each has its own privacy policy, and we encourage you to read them.

Google AdMob (ads)

The free version of LetsCodeJS shows banner ads through Google AdMob. To serve and measure ads, Google may collect your device’s advertising ID, IP address, approximate location derived from the IP address, device and operating-system details, and information about how ads are displayed and interacted with. Ads may be personalized based on this data. Learn more in Google’s advertising policy and how AdMob uses data from apps.

Where required by law (for example in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom), you will be asked for consent before personalized ads are shown. Buying the lifetime unlock removes ads.

Google Play Billing (purchases)

LetsCodeJS offers a single, one-time payment that unlocks the full version for life. There is no subscription and no recurring charge. The payment is processed by Google Play. We never see or store your card or payment details. Google tells the app whether the purchase is valid, and you can restore it on a new device with the same Google account. See Google’s Privacy Policy.

Package and library services

Some features load code from the internet while you work:

  • npm registry (registry.npmjs.org): when you search for packages, your search text is sent to npm. See npm’s privacy policy.
  • esm.sh: used to fetch package files for the editor, for installed packages, and for previews. The package names and versions you request are sent to it.
  • Tailwind CDN (cdn.tailwindcss.com): loaded in previews and exported pages that use Tailwind.

Like any website or CDN, these services can see your IP address and basic request details. We do not control their logs. Some packages may be cached on your device so previews work offline.

Expo (optional)

These features only run when you use them. See Expo’s privacy policy.

  • Expo account sign-in: when you paste an access token, the app sends it to Expo’s API (api.expo.dev) to confirm it is valid and to read your Expo username. We do not receive the token.
  • Device preview for React Native: the app uploads your project’s files and dependency list to Expo Snack. The upload is anonymous (not tied to your Expo account), but anyone who has the resulting link or QR code can open the project. Do not put secrets, API keys or personal information in a project you preview this way.

Features that share your work

  • Live preview over Wi-Fi: the app can serve your project from your phone so another device on the same network can open it. This uses plain HTTP, and anyone on that network who has the address can view the preview while it is running. Use it only on networks you trust, and stop the session when you are done.
  • Export and share: when you export a project, the app creates a file and opens your phone’s share sheet. What happens next depends on the app you choose.
  • Copy to clipboard: copying an error message or a preview link puts it on your clipboard only when you tap the copy button.

Permissions

LetsCodeJS asks only for what its features need:

  • Internet and network state: to load packages, show ads, run previews and check connectivity.
  • Photos and files: only when you choose to add an image or file to a project. You pick the files; the app does not browse your library on its own.

The app does not use your contacts, microphone, camera or precise location.

Retention and deletion

  • On-device data stays until you delete a project, clear the app’s storage in Android settings, or uninstall the app.
  • Expo token: sign out of Expo inside the app to remove it from your device. You can also revoke the token in your Expo account settings.
  • Expo Snack uploads are held by Expo, not by us. To ask for removal, contact Expo.
  • Ad data is held by Google under its own policies.

Your choices and rights

  • Ads: on Android, open Settings > Privacy > Ads (the path varies by device) to reset your advertising ID or opt out of ad personalization. You can also manage ad settings at myadcenter.google.com.
  • Remove ads entirely: buy the one-time lifetime unlock. It is a single payment, not a subscription.
  • Legal rights: depending on where you live (for example under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, the GDPR, or the CCPA), you may have rights to access, correct, delete or restrict the use of your personal information. Because we hold almost no personal information about you, most of it sits with Google, Expo and the other services above. Contact them for data they hold, or contact us and we will help where we can.

Children

LetsCodeJS is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has provided information through the app, contact us and we will help get it removed.

Security

We store sensitive items such as your Expo token and purchase record in your device’s secure storage. No method of storage or transmission is perfectly secure, so we cannot guarantee absolute security. Keeping your device locked and up to date helps protect everything in the app.

Changes to this policy

If we change how LetsCodeJS handles information, we will update this page and the effective date at the top. For significant changes we will also tell you in the app or on the Google Play listing. Continued use after a change means you accept the updated policy.
